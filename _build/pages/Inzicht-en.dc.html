Why live

Your event deserves a bigger audience than the sidelines

Why a livestream makes sense for smaller sports in particular. First what research shows, then what we saw ourselves at the European Cable Wakeboard Championships.

See our own numbers ↓
The problem

A weekend of work. An audience the size of the stands.

Fans are at home

Family, clubmates and followers outside the region see nothing of what happens.

Sponsors get little back

A banner along the course only reaches whoever is there that day.

On Sunday it’s over

No footage, no replay, nothing to sell the next edition with.

After the event

After the last heat, the second life begins

The stream stops, the content doesn’t. From the same footage we make new pieces that keep bringing in reach for weeks, for the event and for the sponsors.

European Cable Wakeboard Championships 2026

After the event, more views came in than the streams got themselves

Together, the three streams got 27,508 views. In the 28 days after, the channel added another 28,500 views. The count has since passed 30,000 views. Research points the same way: most live video watch time happens after the broadcast.
